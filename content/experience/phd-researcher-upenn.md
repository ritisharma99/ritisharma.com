My PhD research sits at the intersection of experimental mechanics and materials biology. Using synchrotron X-ray nano-tomography and radiography at NSLS-II (Brookhaven National Laboratory), I image bone at sub-micron resolution in 3D, capturing how cracks form, propagate, and decelerate within mineralized collagen fibrils.

The central question: why does bone resist fatigue so much better than its constituent materials would suggest? My work provides the first direct 3D evidence that fibril-scale crack bridging and tortuous crack paths are key to that resilience. I also characterize the viscoelastic behavior of these fibrillar structures and collaborate with Drexel University on advanced materials characterization and imaging.

This research has led to two poster award wins (SES 2023, PCMD 2023) and a manuscript currently in review at *Science Advances*.
